About

Evidence first, then everything else

I coordinate sustainability work that has to survive scrutiny — from a client’s board, a research ethics panel, or a grower who has farmed the same field for thirty years.

My route in was practical rather than theoretical: nature restoration weekends and a community garden plot, long before it was a job title. That grounding still shapes how I work — I trust what can be measured on the ground more than what looks good in a slide deck.

Since then I have worked across research, project coordination and operations. The common thread is translation: turning environmental data into decisions people can act on, and turning the concerns of growers, residents and funders into something a research plan can answer.

I care about the ethics of the method as much as the outcome. Consent, transparency about who benefits, and honesty about uncertainty are not paperwork — they are what makes a finding worth acting on.

The work I want most sits at the intersection of regenerative agriculture, biodiversity and climate resilience: the places where a better food system and a healthier landscape turn out to be the same project.

How I work

Three commitments

01

Measure before you promise

A footprint assessment is only useful if the baseline is honest. I would rather report an uncomfortable number than a flattering estimate.

02

Ethics is a method, not a form

Research standards, informed consent and clear data handling belong at the design stage — not in an appendix written afterwards.

03

Plan past the funding cycle

Soil, hedgerows and trust all improve on a longer timescale than a project plan. Design the handover on day one.
